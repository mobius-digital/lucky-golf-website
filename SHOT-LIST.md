## How to shoot the gold

One note that governs most of this list: **the finish has to read as metal, not
as paint.** That means raking light — low sun, or a hard side light in studio.
Flat overhead light at midday is the single most common way these shots fail.

Where a brief says *low sun*, *raking light* or *side light*, it is load-bearing
and not a suggestion.

## Session B · Apparel day — on body

**23 shots.** Thirteen polos and ten hats, one shot each, all **portrait 4:5,
on body**. There is no on-body apparel photography in existence today — every
garment page currently shows a grey box, so this session unblocks 23 pages at
once and is the single biggest win on the list.

Every brief names the light it needs, and several of these patterns **literally
vanish in flat light** — Blackout, Marble and the Baby Blue Cursive hat all say
so explicitly. Read them before booking a grey day.

### Azalea Classic Polo

- [ ] **Photo** — Portrait 4:5, on body, spring light and green surroundings. The reference does the work; the shot should not try to explain it.

### Blackout Blade Polo

- [ ] **Photo** — Portrait 4:5, on body. Raking side light is mandatory — the pattern is tonal and in flat light this photographs as a plain black shirt, which undersells it.

### Contour Classic Polo

- [ ] **Photo** — Portrait 4:5, on body, mid-round. Golfer at the top of the backswing or walking off a tee, shot from slightly below so the collar and shoulder line read. The print has to be legible as texture at this distance.

### Cruiser Classic Polo

- [ ] **Photo** — Portrait 4:5, on body, ideally beside or in an actual cart. The joke lands harder in context, and one close crop is needed for anyone to see what the print is.

### Frost Classic Polo

- [ ] **Photo** — Portrait 4:5, on body, strong sun. White is the hardest color to expose — this brief needs a shot that keeps detail in the fabric rather than blowing the shirt out.

### Gold Carnation Classic Polo

- [ ] **Photo** — Portrait 4:5, on body. Bright natural light — a white ground with fine gold linework goes flat and gray in shade.

### Gold Dust Classic Polo

- [ ] **Photo** — Portrait 4:5, on body, with a gold club in frame. The whole shot is the rhyme between the flecks and the finish on the club.

### Marble Classic Polo

- [ ] **Photo** — Portrait 4:5, on body, moving. The pattern needs motion and raking light to read — a static front-on shot in flat light will lose the whole effect.

### Nightshade Classic Polo

- [ ] **Photo** — Portrait 4:5, on body. High-contrast print on a dark ground needs a bright background to separate — sky or open fairway, never trees.

### Shadow Classic Polo

- [ ] **Photo** — Portrait 4:5, on body. Needs one close crop as a second frame — the clover reveal is the entire idea and it is invisible at full-body distance.

### Signature Black Classic Polo

- [ ] **Photo** — Portrait 4:5, on body, off-course as much as on. This is the one that leaves the golf course, so photograph it doing that — clubhouse, evening light.

### Spot Blade Polo

- [ ] **Photo** — Portrait 4:5, on body, hard summer light. Mint goes muddy in shade and gray under cloud; this one needs the sun.

### Swirl Blade Polo

- [ ] **Photo** — Portrait 4:5, on body. The blade collar is half the product — the crop has to include the neckline, and it has to be lit so the absence of a placket is obvious.

### Baby Blue Cursive Hat
