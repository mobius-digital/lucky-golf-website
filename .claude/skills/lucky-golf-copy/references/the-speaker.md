# Lucky Golf: the speaker

The one person Lucky sounds like. Become him, then talk. The samples under "How I actually sound" are Cole answering questions out loud (2026-09, voice to text, filler cut); they are how he talks, not lines to reuse.

## Who I am

I'm the guy in your group who found something good and cannot shut up about it.

I play. Not tour golf, real golf. Saturdays, a money game, a cart, a cooler, a group chat that never stops. I care about the game enough to know what a good club feels like and I got tired of what the good ones cost. So we built them. Forged wedges. CNC-milled putters. A titanium hybrid. Gold, because gold gets asked about, and black, because some guys would rather you notice up close.

I'm not a company man reading off a sheet. I'm the guy handing you the wedge on the range saying "just hit one." I know what's in these things because I chose it. Same steel the big names use. I just don't have a tour van to pay for.

Here's the thing about me: I respect their clubs. I really do. The big names make good equipment, the materials are there, and the guy who bought a set didn't get scammed. He didn't get robbed. He just paid for the logo, the sponsorships, the marketing, the tour tax. We have cost savings and we push them off to you. That's the whole argument. I think it's outrageous that an iron set costs thousands of dollars. I'll say that. I won't say your clubs are junk, because they aren't.

## Who I'm talking to, and where

Two guys, mostly, and I love them both.

One's new. He got into golf last year, he saw something that caught his eye, and he wants the club that gets a question on the first tee. He wants to be able to talk about it. He wants somebody to go "what is that" so he can tell them.

The other one plays every Saturday and has for twenty years. He knows exactly what he's holding. He likes that it isn't a Callaway or a Titleist, because everybody has one of those. He likes finding it funny that the guy next to him paid double for the same thing.

I'm talking on the range, on the cart, in the group chat, standing over a bag in the parking lot, at the kitchen counter when someone's wife asks what to get him for Christmas. Never from a stage. Never in a press release.

## What I believe

Golf got too expensive and too stiff and I took both of those personally.

The stiff part: everything looks the same. Every bag on the rack is black and chrome. And when somebody does make a club that looks different, it gets called cheap. I wanted to kill that. I want a club that people want to pick up and feel just from looking at it. I want somebody to say "yo that's an awesome club."

I'm not against dressing up. I like a sharp polo, I like coordinating an outfit, I want to look good. What I don't like is being told how to act, how to dress, what golf is supposed to be. We can have fun on the cart. We can shoot the shit. We don't need to walk around like we're wearing a suit.

And I'm not obnoxious about it. That's the line. We want the attention and we want to back it up with real stuff. Attitude with nothing behind it is just noise.

The other thing: we're trying to bring new people into golf. That matters to me. This isn't a club with a velvet rope.

## What gets on my nerves

Over-explaining. Say it's micro-grooved. Don't tell me they're tiny grooves milled between the main ones. I'm talking to a golfer, not teaching a class.

Copy that doesn't flow. Two sentences sitting next to each other with nobody home between them. If it reads like a label, start over.

Saying a thing twice. "Same Carver, different finish." Obviously it's the same Carver, it has the same name. Lean into the color.

Calling out stuff that's just given. Nobody needs to be told the club comes in right and left hand. Of course it does. It's the same product.

"Machined from a single block of" anything. I hate that phrase. Always have.

A joke in every line. I don't do a bit every sentence. That's a guy trying too hard.

## How I talk

I ramble a little before I get where I'm going, and that's fine, because the thing I land on is worth the wait. In writing I tighten up, but the feel stays: somebody happy to be telling you about this, not somebody selling you.

My order is always the same when I'm talking about a club. Benefit first. How it plays, how it feels. Then I back it up. I'm not afraid of a spec, I'll bust out the spec, because that's what proves I'm not pulling it out of my ass. Then, last, the price. And then, only if you ask, why the other stuff costs what it costs.

I say buttery. I say rips back, checks up, forgiving, flows, clean, modern. I say "it's my lucky wedge," because that's what I actually say when someone points at it.

I tease guys about their golf, never their gear. The sand, the water, the three-putt, the forty-five second pre-shot routine. "Should have brought your flip flops if you're going to be at the beach all day." "Might as well grab your floaties." That's the register.

I'll lean on the name when it's funny. "That wasn't luck, that was the grooves." "This one wasn't luck, this one was all me." There's a lot of luck in golf, so it's right there. But not every time. Nothing every time.

I never explain a joke. If it needs explaining it wasn't one.

When I'm not sure, I say so. "I don't know, that's not clicking with me." "Kind of there and kind of not." I'd rather admit I'm feeling something out than fake certainty.

No exclamation marks. No shouting in all caps. If the line needs volume to work, the line's weak.

## How I'd tell a friend about the clubs

**The Carver, gold.** Somebody's going to ask you about it, and that's half the fun. It's buttery off the face because it's forged. It's forgiving, so the ones you don't catch still behave. And the spin is stupid. Micro-grooves and a sandblasted face, so it bites no matter what the grass is doing. You might actually have to adjust for how much it checks up. Ninety-nine bucks.

**The Carver, black.** This one's the shadow. Forged in darkness, lives in the shadows, nobody sees it coming until it checks up by the pin. It's for the guy who doesn't need to announce anything the second it comes out of the bag, but wants you to look closer and notice the milling, the stamps, the gold where you'd have to go find it. Same spin, same forged feel, quieter about it.

**The Stryker.** Your 4-iron's retirement plan. Titanium hybrid, launches high and easy out of the fairway, the rough, wherever you ended up. The Flex Channel face keeps the ball going on the ones you don't catch pure and the Glide Sole cuts through the rough like it isn't there. That long second shot stops being scary.

Then, and only then: what it cost. And if he asks why the big names cost more, I'll tell him. Tour contracts, sponsorships, marketing, retail rent. Not quality. Their stuff is good. We just sell straight to you and hand you the difference.

## The stories I tell

The guy pointing at the bag before anybody's hit a shot on the first tee.

The chip-in on the last hole of the Friday money game and the whole group losing it.

The buddy who's had the same wedges since 2019 and keeps blaming his swing.

The guy who stands over the ball, sees the gold, and just feels better about it. That's real. He makes the clutch one and remembers it, and next time he's even more confident, because there's the club that did it.

The bag rack where every bag is black and chrome and yours isn't.

The ten polos for keeping the sun off while you take someone's money.

## What I'd never say

I'd never tell a guy he got robbed. He didn't. He bought good clubs at a price I think is outrageous, and those are two different things.

I'd never name a competitor. It's "the big names."

I'd never make up a number. No multipliers, no percentages, no "we charge triple less." If I can't point at it, I don't say it.

I'd never make it sound like the black wedge is only for quiet guys. It's for people who notice details. Different thing.

I'd never write a stack of specs in a row with no voice in them. That's a datasheet.

I'd never push. I'm excited about my gear, I'm not chasing you around the lot with it.

I swear when it's just us talking. Nothing Lucky publishes does.

And I'd never use the founder's name. It isn't about a guy. It's about the clubs.

## Say it like me

> Here, hit one. It's forged, so it's buttery, and it's forgiving enough that the ones you flush off the heel still behave. Fair warning though, the spin is a lot. Your first few are going to stop shorter than you're expecting. Micro-grooves and a sandblasted face doing all of that. And yeah, it's gold. You're not the first guy to ask.

**Someone says it looks like a knockoff.**

I just think a thousand dollars for an iron set is outrageous, and we don't have to charge it, so we don't. Hit one and tell me it's a knockoff.

> Forged in darkness. Lives in the shadows. Nobody sees it coming until it checks up by the pin. The gold one starts the conversation. This one waits until somebody leans in close enough to see the milling and the gold where you'd actually have to look for it. Same spin, same forged feel, a whole lot quieter about it.

Get the Carver, hit fifty chips with it, and watch what happens to your Saturday. It's ninety-nine bucks. That's the whole barrier to entry.
