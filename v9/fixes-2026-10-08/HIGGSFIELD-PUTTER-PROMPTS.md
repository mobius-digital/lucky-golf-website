# Higgsfield prompts: putter tech clips (Takomo Lumo style)

**What Takomo's three clips are:** 6 to 10 s each, 16:9, dark charcoal studio, one slow camera move, a single soft light sweeping across the steel. Clip 2 adds thin drawn lines (balance point). No music, no text in the video. They loop on the page.

**How to run each one**
- Mode: image to video. Start frame = the studio photo named below (Shopify product media, full size).
- Length 6 to 8 s, 16:9, 1080p. Generate 3 or 4 takes per clip and keep the one where the head doesn't change shape.
- **Reject any take where** the clover logo, LUCKY lettering, sight line, neck or head outline changes, or the gold turns yellow-plastic.
- The drawn lines (clip 2) go on after, in CapCut, or I add them on the website in code. Don't ask the AI for lines or text.

**Paste this block in front of every prompt:**
> Photorealistic product film of a gold, fully CNC-milled stainless steel golf putter head. Keep the putter EXACTLY as in the reference image: same shape, same neck, same green clover logo, same engraving, same proportions. No new logos, no text, no people. Dark charcoal seamless studio background, soft falloff. One slow, smooth camera move only. Brushed satin gold metal with fine milling marks. Cinematic, shallow depth of field, 24 fps, no flicker.

## Eclipse Blade

**1. Milled face (start frame: the face-on studio shot with the clover on the face)**
> Macro close-up of the putter face. A thin strip of soft white light sweeps slowly from heel to toe across the milled diamond face pattern, catching every milling mark. Camera creeps forward very slightly. Background stays dark.

**2. Built for your arc (start frame: the heel/toe-down product shot, head with neck visible)**
> The putter hangs from the shaft in mid-air against the dark background and slowly rotates on its own until the toe points toward the floor, then settles and holds still. Smooth, weighted motion, like a pendulum coming to rest. Camera static.
*(Then add: a thin gold arc line showing the toe swinging down. That's the "strong toe hang, built for an arcing stroke" moment.)*

**3. Top line at address (start frame: the top-down / address studio shot)**
> Slow overhead push-in on the putter head at address. A soft light passes along the top line and the single white sight line. Ends perfectly still, square to camera.

## Eclipse Mallet

**1. Milled face** same as the Blade 1, start frame = Mallet face close-up.

**2. Built for your arc (start frame: the fang mallet from behind, wings visible)**
> The mallet hangs from the shaft in mid-air and slowly rotates until it settles about halfway between face-up and toe-down, then holds still. Smooth, weighted motion. Camera static.

**3. Fang wings (start frame: the top-down shot showing the two wings and the centre gap)**
> Slow orbit from behind the mallet toward the golfer's view at address. Light glides along both rear wings and the centre line. Ends square to camera, ball-position gap framed in the middle.
