The first complaint was how golf looked. Walk down any range and every bag's full of the same silver and black, with the same logos, and the game had started to feel as stiff as the gear. Golf's supposed to be the best four hours of your week. We wanted clubs with some personality, the kind people ask about.

The second was the price. Good wedges cost a lot, and when we looked into why, it wasn't the steel. The big names use good materials and solid engineering, and then add tour contracts, retail shelf space and ad campaigns on top. We figured we'd use the same materials, skip everything stacked on top and sell straight to golfers.

If people are going to stare at your wedge, it might as well be gold.

So we made a gold wedge

That's all the plan was at first. One wedge, forged, in gold, and good enough around the green that you'd keep it in the bag long after the novelty wore off. It caught on, more clubs followed, and today it's the Carver: 1020 forged carbon steel with a micro-grooved, sandblasted face that spins more than most golfers are used to.

What you'll find on this blog

Mostly help. How to get out of a buried bunker lie, how to stop chunking chips, which wedge does what, and the odd piece on the mental side of a game that loves to mess with your head. Some of it comes from golf pros we've worked with, and some from our own practice sessions.

Stick around, and if something here saves you a stroke, we'd love it if you told your buddies where you read it.
