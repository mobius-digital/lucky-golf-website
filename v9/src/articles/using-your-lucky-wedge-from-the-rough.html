Nobody wants to be in the rough, and chipping out of it is where a lot of rounds get away. The grass catches the club on the way down, slows it and can twist the face. Chips already tempt you to decelerate, which is exactly how the chili dip happens, and thick grass makes the temptation worse.

Colin McCarthy, a PGA professional and coach, has an easy way to cut down the friction as the club goes through the grass. It takes less time to watch than your pre-shot routine.

Read the lie before you pick the shot

Look at how the ball's sitting before anything else. If it's sitting up on top of the grass, you can nip it almost like a fairway chip, just be careful not to slide under it. If it's settled down, there's grass behind it that you'll have to go through, and that changes everything about the shot.

Getting through the thick stuff

  • Open the face a touch and grip a little firmer. The grass wants to close the face, so give yourself a head start and don't let it twist.
  • Swing a bit steeper. A steeper path means less grass between the club and the ball.
  • Commit to the speed. The worst thing you can do in the rough is slow down. Pick a smaller backswing if you need to, then accelerate through.
  • Plan for run. The ball won't spin much, so land it earlier and let it roll to the hole.

The rough doesn't care how pretty the swing is. It only cares that you didn't slow down.
