A normal bunker shot has one rule: open the face and splash the sand out. A buried lie flips that on its head. Open the face here and the club bounces off the top of the sand, and the ball stays exactly where it was, only now you're lying two.

Colin McCarthy, a PGA professional and coach, took his Lucky wedge into a bunker with the ball buried deep in the sand. The video's short and a bit tongue in cheek, and the point stands: no matter how bad it's sitting, there's a way out.

Square it up and swing hard

  • Square the face, or close it a touch. You want the leading edge to dig, because the ball's sitting below the surface.
  • Ball back of center, weight forward. It sets up a steep swing that goes down into the sand instead of skimming across it.
  • Hit hard, about an inch or two behind the ball. Chop down and don't worry about the follow-through. The club can stay in the sand.
  • Plan for roll. It'll come out low and hot with almost no spin. Land it short and let it run to the hole.

Out and on the green is a win. Anything closer is a bonus.

Get the big number out of play

The disaster from a plugged lie isn't a long putt. It's the second shot from the same bunker. Commit to hitting down hard, take your medicine if the green runs away from you, and most of the time the damage stops at a bogey.
