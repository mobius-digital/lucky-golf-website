Pick a number

"Get better" isn't a goal. Breaking 100 is. So is breaking 90 by the end of the season, or never three-putting more than twice a round. A number tells you what to work on and lets you know when you've done it, which is half the fun.

Take a lesson before you need ten

Nearly every tour player has a coach, and they're the best golfers on the planet. A good teacher spots in five minutes what you'd spend a whole season guessing at, whether that's your grip, your setup or the reason your chips come off the toe. One lesson on the basics saves you from grooving a habit you'll have to un-learn later.

Practice with a plan

The range is where most of us go to hit drivers and feel good. The pros spend a huge share of their time from a hundred yards in, because that's where the strokes are. Split your next session: a third on full swings, the rest on chipping, pitching and putting. It's less fun for the first twenty minutes and a lot more fun on the scorecard.

Keep some simple stats while you're at it: fairways, greens, putts and how often you get up and down. After a few rounds you'll know exactly where your strokes are going, and it's rarely where you'd guess.

"The more I practice, the luckier I get." The line's usually credited to Gary Player, and we couldn't agree more.

Play with people who are better than you

You'll pick up more in one round with a single-digit player than in a month of watching videos. Watch how they plan a hole, where they miss on purpose and how little fuss they make about a bad shot. Most good players are happy to share, and you'll find your own game rising to meet theirs.

Keep your head

This one's close to our hearts, because it's why Lucky exists. Golf's frustrating, and you'll get as many bad breaks as good ones. The golfers who improve are the ones still enjoying it on the back nine, so give yourself a break when the ball finds a divot. If you want more on this, we wrote a whole piece on the mental game.
