From the fairway

  1. The full wedge. It's your stock shot from inside a full swing. Ball in the middle of your stance and a smooth swing that finishes balanced, then learn exactly how far it carries with every wedge you've got.
  2. The knockdown. For when the wind's up or the pin's tucked. Move the ball back an inch, keep your hands ahead, shorten the backswing and hold the finish low. It'll stay under the wind and check when it lands.
  3. The pitch from the rough. Grass gets between the face and the ball, so expect less spin and more run. Open the face a touch, swing a little steeper and commit to the speed. Slowing down's what gets the club stuck. We've gone deeper on it here.

Around the green

  1. The pitch. Thirty to sixty yards, where a lot of scores quietly leak. Let your chest turn through the shot instead of flicking with your hands, and don't let the club slow down before you finish.
  2. The one-hop-and-stop. The shot everyone wants on camera. Ball slightly back, crisp contact with the ball first, and a clean, dry face. Hit it right and it'll take one bounce and sit down.
  3. The bump and run. Use a pitching or gap wedge, set up like a long putt and make a putting stroke. Land it just on the green and let it roll. It's the least exciting shot here and it saves the most strokes.
  4. The flop. Open the face first, then take your grip. Wide stance, ball forward, and a full, committed swing that slides under the ball. It won't work from a bad lie, and it won't work if you bail on it.

The flop gets the replays. The bump and run gets the pars.

From the sand

  1. The splash. Open the face, aim to hit the sand an inch or two behind the ball and keep swinging to a full finish. You don't touch the ball at all. The sand throws it out.
  2. The buried lie. Square or slightly close the face, ball back, and hit down hard just behind it. It'll come out low with no spin and run, so play for that. There's a video on it here.

The one nobody practices

  1. The bellied wedge. Ball resting against the collar of the rough, where a putter would snag. Take your sand wedge, hover it so the leading edge lines up with the middle of the ball and make a putting stroke. It'll roll out like a putt, and your playing partners will ask where you learned it.
