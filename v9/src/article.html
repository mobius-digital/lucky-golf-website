Are your wedges forged?

Picking a wedge puts you in front of a choice most golfers never get explained to them: forged or cast. It changes the feel, and it changes the price.

Casting is the cheap way, and there is nothing sinister about it. Molten steel goes into a mould, cools, comes out as a head that needs very little finishing. It is fast, it is repeatable, and it is how most of the wedges on a pro shop wall are made.

Forging starts with a solid billet of steel and beats it into shape under pressure. It takes longer, it wastes more material, and it needs a person to finish the head afterwards. What you get for that is a tighter grain structure in the metal, which is the reason a forged wedge feels the way it does at impact: softer, with more information coming back up the shaft about where on the face you caught it.

A cast wedge tells you the ball has gone. A forged one tells you how.

Does it actually matter to your score?

Honestly, not on a full swing. Hit a 56 from a hundred yards and the difference is smaller than the difference between two golf balls. Where it shows up is the shots around the green, where you are trying to control how far something goes by feel rather than by a number, and the club telling you the truth helps.

If you never chip and you never play a bunker shot, buy the cheap one. If you spend half your round inside a hundred yards, like almost everybody does, feel is not a luxury.

So why is forged usually expensive?

Because the process costs more and because the brands charging for it also pay for tour contracts, retail shelf space and a marketing department. The steel does not know about any of that. Ours is 1020 forged carbon steel with a CNC-milled face, sold direct, at $99.

How to tell what you already own

  • Look for the word forged stamped on the head. Brands that forge say so, because it costs them more.
  • Cast heads often have a slightly softer, rounder edge where the sole meets the face, because the mould leaves it that way.
  • If the spec sheet says 431 or 17-4 stainless, it is almost certainly cast. Carbon steel in the 1020 or 1025 range is usually forged.

None of this makes a cast wedge a bad club. It makes it a different one, at a price that should reflect that, and often does not.
