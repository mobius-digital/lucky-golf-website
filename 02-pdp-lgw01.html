The argument

Most rounds are decided inside a hundred yards.

That's where the strokes go, and it's usually not the swing. It's a wedge that came free with a set, with a face that stopped gripping two seasons ago, doing a job it was never really built for.

The Carver was built for that job specifically. Forged rather than cast, so it feels soft and tells you exactly where on the face you caught it. Milled and sandblasted, so it holds the ball long enough to spin it. Weighted differently at every loft, because a 60° flop and a 50° full swing are not the same shot.

It costs $99 because we sell it to you directly and we don't pay anyone on tour to play it. That's the whole explanation. More on the pricing.

How it's built

Where the money went

Forged, not cast

1020 soft carbon steel. It feels buttery at impact and gives you real feedback on a miss.

Milled grooves

CNC-cut, with micro-milling between them. More texture on the ball means more spin.

Sandblasted face

Friction across the whole face, not just in the grooves. It grips out of wet grass too.

Weighted by loft

Higher lofts run toe-weighted for finesse. Lower lofts sit lower for cleaner full swings.

Which loft

Start with the wedge you already carry

Wedges work in gaps. Four to six degrees between clubs keeps your distances even and stops two wedges going the same yardage. Tell us the loft of your pitching wedge and we'll do the arithmetic.
