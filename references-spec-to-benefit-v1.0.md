CONSTRUCTION AND MATERIALS
1020 Forged Carbon Steel
Products: All wedges (LGW01, LGW02 Gold, LGW02 Black)

Technically: 1020 is a lower-carbon, softer grade of steel than the cast stainless used in most mass-market wedges. Forging compresses the metal under heat and pressure, producing a tighter, more uniform grain structure than casting. Softer steel absorbs more energy at impact and deforms microscopically before returning. That absorption is what the golfer registers as "feel." The uniform grain means that feel is consistent across the entire face.

For the golfer: Buttery, solid sensation at impact. No hollowness. Feedback is immediate and honest: you know exactly how you struck the ball the moment you hit it. On mishits, the feel stays smooth rather than harsh or thin. The entire face feels responsive, and off-center strikes still produce good spin and accuracy.


CNC-Milled from a Single Block
Products: LGP01 Tracer Blade, LGP02 Tracer Mallet

Technically: The entire putter head is carved from one piece of stainless steel using precision CNC tooling. No casting, no welding, no assembled components, no hollow cavity. Internal density is uniform throughout. At impact, vibration travels through continuous material rather than bouncing between joined surfaces.

For the golfer: A muted, solid sensation at impact. No ring, no ping, no vibration buzzing through the hands. The ball comes off the face with a low-resistance, buttery feel. On off-center strikes, you feel where you missed, but it doesn't punish you. Honest feedback without harshness.


431 Stainless Steel (Blade) vs. 304 Stainless Steel (Mallet)
Products: LGP01 Tracer Blade (431), LGP02 Tracer Mallet (304)

Technically: 431 is a harder martensitic stainless. 304 is a slightly softer austenitic stainless. Both machine cleanly for CNC milling. In a milled putter, the difference in feel between these two grades is marginal.

For the golfer: Both feel solid and muted. The milling process and single-block construction drive the feel experience, not the alloy. Most golfers could not reliably distinguish the two by feel alone. The steel grade is a spec-table fact, not a headline.


Titanium
Products: LGH01 Stryker

Technically: Titanium has a higher strength-to-weight ratio than steel. A titanium head can be made larger or with thinner walls while staying within target weight, allowing mass redistribution for forgiveness and launch. The material also flexes differently at impact, supporting the Flex Channel face design.

For the golfer: A lighter-feeling head with easy launch and solid contact. Titanium allows the Stryker to deliver ball speed and forgiveness from a compact shape. It works in partnership with the Flex Channel face and Glide Sole rather than standing alone as a benefit.


FACE FEATURES
Dual-Texture Face
Products: All wedges (LGW01, LGW02 Gold, LGW02 Black)

Two surface treatments work together on top of the standard grooves:

Micro-grooved texture between and around the grooves adds friction points across the face, increasing total contact area. This is the primary spin driver. It produces noticeably high spin rates and is most responsible for the aggressive check on the green.

Sandblasted finish across the face adds roughness that prevents the ball from sliding on partial contact (slightly off-center strikes) and in wet conditions where moisture would reduce grip on a smooth face.

For the golfer: Spin that shows up on every shot, not just perfect ones. The ball checks hard on centered strikes, still checks on off-center strikes, and holds its spin in wet conditions. One-hop stops, aggressive check. In testing, spin numbers were consistently higher than expected. The golfer doesn't think about two textures. They think: this wedge spins.


Flex Channel Face
Products: LGH01 Stryker

Technically: Channels machined into the titanium face reduce stiffness in specific zones, allowing the face to compress slightly on contact and spring back. On centered strikes, this rebound transfers more energy to the ball, producing higher ball speed. On off-center strikes, the flex recovers ball speed that would normally be lost to a rigid face, producing a higher, more stable ball flight.

For the golfer: More distance from the same swing, especially on the shots you don't catch perfectly. The centered strike goes far. The toe or heel strike goes farther than expected. The bad swings don't cost as much.

SOLE AND GRIND FEATURES
K Grind
Products: LGW01 Carver, LGW02 Carver Black

Technically: A wider, flatter sole profile with a standard leading edge. The wider sole distributes turf contact across more surface area, resisting digging and promoting a consistent bottom-out point. Sits flat behind the ball at address.

For the golfer: The no-surprises wedge. Set up square, swing through, take your divot, and the club does what you expect. Built for the golfer who strikes cleanly and plays primarily from standard lies: fairway, rough, fringe. Great for full swings and straightforward short-game shots.


S Grind
Products: LGW02 Carver Gold

Technically: A narrower, more relieved sole with a reshaped leading edge. Less surface area contacting the turf lets the club sit lower behind the ball when the face is opened. The reshaped leading edge slides under the ball in tight lies where a wider sole would skip or catch. Reduced bounce effect in open-face positions means less resistance on finesse shots.

For the golfer: The versatility wedge. Handles tight lies, awkward angles, open-face lobs, and bunker shots where you need to slide the club under the ball. When the ball isn't sitting clean and you need to get creative, the S Grind lets you work the club without fighting it. More shots from more places.


Glide Sole
Products: LGH01 Stryker

Technically: Sole geometry shaped to reduce friction through turf and rough. From the fairway, prevents the leading edge from catching and decelerating before impact. From the rough, lets the club slide through grass rather than getting grabbed and twisted.

For the golfer: Consistent contact whether the ball is sitting up, sitting down, or buried. The club doesn't fight the ground on the way to the ball. Clean strikes from more lies.

WEIGHTING
Consistent 300g Head Weight Across All Wedge Lofts
Products: All wedges (50° through 60°)

Technically: Unlike manufacturers who vary head weight by loft, Lucky holds weight constant at 300g (±3g) across all lofts. Swing weight and feel are consistent as the golfer moves between wedges.

For the golfer: Every wedge in the bag swings the same. The transition from your gap wedge to your lob wedge doesn't require an adjustment in tempo or feel. If you've dialed in your 56°, the 60° feels the same in your hands.


Putter Head Weights: Blade (385g) vs. Mallet (365g)
Products: LGP01 Tracer Blade, LGP02 Tracer Mallet

Technically: The blade's heavier head provides stability through the stroke and resists face manipulation. The mallet's lighter head (relative to its larger shape) achieves stability through geometry (higher MOI from mass distribution) rather than raw weight.

For the golfer: Both feel stable and controlled. The blade feels planted and deliberate. The mallet feels balanced and easy to swing on its arc. The weight difference supports each head shape's stroke style rather than creating a dramatically different feel.


CLAIMS TO AVOID
These apply across all products and all copy formats.

Don't claim forged is more durable. 1020 is softer by design. Grooves and face texture wear faster than harder alloys. The softness is the feature. Don't pretend it lasts longer.
Don't use the word "cheap." Use "priced honestly" or let the price speak.
Don't claim "premium" as a marketing word. Show it through materials and construction. Don't label it.
Don't say "game-changing," "disruptive," or "affordable luxury."
Don't compare to brands by name. Never "better than Vokey" or "outperforms Cleveland." Let the customer make the connection.
Don't make specific handicap claims. No "drop 5 strokes." Keep score language aspirational: "save more pars," "fewer bogeys."
Don't say "tour-proven" without an actual tour player. "Tour-quality construction" describing materials and process is fine. "Tour-proven" requires proof.
Don't claim durability on forged wedges. The grooves wear faster than harder alloys. That's the tradeoff for the feel.
Don't overclaim 431 vs. 304. The feel difference isn't dramatic. The milling matters more than the alloy. Be honest about that.
Don't imply Lucky invented channel-face technology. Flex Channel is Lucky's name for its implementation. Describe what it does, not that it's a first.
Don't lead with "milled from a single block" as a headline. The phrase has been overused across the putter market. Describe what the construction delivers, not the process name.
